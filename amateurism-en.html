The problem we found

Quitting isn't your fault.
The system is broken.

You've tried singing, painting, dancing — but were you trying to become a singer, a painter, a dancer? You just wanted to have fun. The problem is, most hobby classes weren't designed for you.

"We don't want another source of stress —
we want a place where we can actually unwind."
👶

Built for children, not adults

Most hobby classes follow a children's curriculum — drilling the same basics over and over until you forget why you showed up in the first place.

🏆

Obsessed with turning pro

Exams, recitals, competitions — has anyone ever asked: what if you just want to play around, with no intention of becoming an expert?

📅

Monthly passes that trap you

Travel, overtime, illness — the credits are already gone, and the teacher will message the group asking why you didn't show up last week.

😤

Judged and compared

Progress charts, public critiques, comparisons with other students. Being a "beginner" feels like something to apologise for.

⏱

Schedules that don't fit your life

Fixed time slots, long-term booking commitments — almost impossible to sync with a busy adult life. It's not that you don't want to come. You just can't.

😶

Hours in, nothing to show for it

Lots of practice, still feels terrible. Hobby classes seem to drain your enthusiasm rather than ignite it.
